# What the code did before the fix (8b18922130), read from the code
- Who gets a block change: InstanceContainer#UNSAFE_setBlock holds the instance monitor and the chunk write-lock, changes the
  palette (DynamicChunk#setBlock invalidates the chunk packet cache inside that lock), then, still under the lock, sends the
  BlockChangePacket to the chunk's viewers (Chunk#sendPacketToViewers). The viewers come from EntityTrackerImpl.ChunkView#references:
  every player of the instance whose position is within effectiveViewDistance() chunks of the chunk (Chebyshev distance). There is no
  per-player set of sent chunks behind it. The client drops a block change for a chunk it does not hold (as the lighting session's
  docs/DESIGN-M3.md section 28.2 row on the window states; not checked against the 26.2 jar in this round); the test models the
  client that way.
- When a player starts to get the block changes of a chunk: as soon as its position is in reach. On spawn the player is in the
  tracker before its ring is queued (super.setInstance, then the ring); on a move the position and the ring change in the same
  refreshCoordinate. So the player gets the changes of a chunk before the chunk is sent, the client drops them, and the chunk packet
  has to carry every change made before it reaches the connection.
- When the content of a chunk packet is fixed: DynamicChunk and LightingChunk hand out their CachedPacket; PlayerSocketConnection
  serializes it on the write thread when it reaches the head of the queue (CachedPacket#computeCache under the chunk locks), after it
  was queued: no window. SynxChunk overlay mode takes a snapshot under its own write-lock in getFullDataPacket and returns it; handle
  mode returns segment 1 after reading the mode without a lock: the content is fixed when getFullDataPacket returns, before
  sendPacket queues it. A world flag (WorldFlagsImpl on the tick scheduler thread) or any setBlock between the two queues its
  BlockChange ahead of the chunk; the client drops it, then gets the old chunk, and keeps it while the chunk stays in its ring. The
  test connection (TestConnection) extracts the packet inside sendPacket, so it has the same window in tests.
- Lock order: sendPendingChunks holds chunkQueueLock for the whole batch; SynxChunk#getFullDataPacket, and createChunkPacket when a
  test connection serializes, take the chunk write-lock inside it, without a bound. A thread that holds a chunk lock and queues chunks
  for that player (Chunk#sendChunk takes chunkQueueLock) waits for the send while the send waits for it (TC-PROD-676 on the code before
  the fix: until the test's holder gives up after 5 s).
- The chunk update limiter (synaxis.player.chunk-update-limiter-history-size, default 5) keeps the ring center when the player enters
  one of its last 5 chunks; updateViewDistance already measures from the center (REQ-M2-014), ChunkView measured from the position. A
  chunk on the far side of the ring is then held by the client but out of reach of the position: its block changes are not sent to
  that player, and nothing resends them (TC-PROD-677 on the code before the fix).

# The fix (a6155af745)
- Player#sendPendingChunks takes the packet and hands it to the connection under the chunk write-lock (tryLockWriteLock, at most 1 ms,
  constant CHUNK_LOCK_WAIT); a chunk whose lock is not free goes back to the queue and the batch ends, the next tick tries again.
  PlayerChunkLoadEvent is dispatched after the lock is released. The order stays chunkQueueLock outside, chunk write-lock inside.
- EntityTrackerImpl.ChunkView#references: the viewers are the players whose ring has the chunk (new Player#isChunkInRing, the same
  test as isOutOfClientRange: center chunksLoadedByClient, radius effectiveViewDistance()).

# Effect on the chunk cap of wave 5 and on cost
- synaxis.chunk-queue.max-per-tick (engine 64, shard host 4) is unchanged: the credit of a chunk not sent stays in pendingChunkCount,
  which the next tick clamps to the cap again, so no burst above it. The chunk put back was taken from the queue a moment before and is
  in the ring (checked before the lock), so the bound of RUN-070, queue <= (2r + 1)^2 before every send, holds.
- One write-lock acquire and release per chunk sent. On a socket connection the locked section holds getFullDataPacket (a field read
  for DynamicChunk; the snapshot SynxChunk already took under the same lock) and the queue offer, no serialization.
- What the send can wait for: threads holding the chunk's read- or write-lock: block lookups (Instance#getBlock takes the read-lock
  on every call, physics and collision use it), the chunk tick's snapshot of its ticking handlers, a write thread serializing the
  chunk packet the first time after a change, the relight snapshot (SynxRelight), the overlay encode (SynxChunk#encodeOverlay holds the
  write-lock while it encodes; docs/DESIGN-M3.md estimates about 0.5 ms). Each chunk of a batch waits up to 1 ms on its own, and
  only the wait it does not win ends the batch: the waits of one player in one tick add up to about (chunks of the batch + 1) x 1 ms,
  about 65 ms at the engine default of 64 chunks a tick and about 5 ms at the 4 of the shard host, and the players of one tick thread
  add up. The chunk put back is the closest to the center and comes out first the next tick: a chunk whose lock is held at every try
  holds back every chunk of that player behind it. (An earlier version of this file said at most one wait of 1 ms per player and
  tick, which the code did not do; the review found it, see "Review rework".) Measured in TC-PROD-676: the other thread got the queue
  lock after 1,196 to 1,382 us over 16 runs (02, 03, 04 and the earlier holds).
- Not measured: how often a send waits or defers under load (no load test or JMH in this round).

# Review rework (c193a22479), 2026-10-06 22:09 to 22:45 (UTC+8)
The review of the docs commit 883f222f25 found the bound above stated wrong and the starvation understated, and four smaller points.
Chosen: change the code (not only the docs), because the per-chunk wait let one player hold its tick thread for about 65 ms at the
engine default, and the head-of-line block left a player without every chunk behind one busy chunk.
- Player#sendPendingChunks: the waits of a batch share CHUNK_LOCK_WAIT (each tryLockWriteLock waits for what is left of it, a try
  without a wait once it is spent); a chunk not locked in time is deferred, the batch goes on with the chunks behind it and puts the
  deferred ones back in the queue once it is done; a deferral takes the place of a chunk sent, so a batch tries at most as many
  chunks as it may send; the position sync after the first chunks needs a batch that sent a chunk (batchSize > 0). The order of the
  locks (queue lock outside, chunk write-lock inside), the bound of the queue (trimChunkQueue) and the per-tick cap are unchanged;
  no wait is unbounded.
- Bound now: the waits of one player in one tick add up to at most 1 ms plus the scheduling of one timed wait, whatever the batch
  size; N players of one tick thread at most N ms. A chunk whose lock is held at every try keeps being deferred, one place of the
  batch and at most 1 ms a tick, without holding back the chunks behind it.
- Test class (5 tests): TC-PROD-675 also checks that each ChunkDataPacket is handed to the connection under its chunk write-lock
  (CheckedPlayer, a Player subclass that watches sendPacket); TC-PROD-679 adds the head-of-line case (6 of 7 chunks of a column held

# Second review rework (68d6b07ea2), 2026-10-06 23:05 to 23:40 (UTC+8)
The review of c193a22479 found two faults in the put-back of the deferred chunks and three points in the docs.
- A PlayerChunkLoadEvent listener runs on the sending thread under the reentrant queue lock. One that calls sendChunk for a chunk the
  batch has deferred queues it again; the batch dequeues it again and defers it again, and the loop after the batch put both copies
  back without a check: chunkQueue one entry more than queuedChunks (trimChunkQueue only counts queuedChunks), the chunk sent twice.
- The put-back ran after the loop, inside the try block: an exception from getFullDataPacket or sendPacket left the batch before it
  and lost the chunks deferred so far.
- Fix: Player#sendPendingChunks declares the deferred list before it takes the queue lock and puts the deferred chunks back in the
  finally block that releases it, each one only if queuedChunks does not hold it yet (the check sendChunk makes). The chunk whose
  packet threw is not put back, as before: one that throws every time would be dequeued and throw again every tick
  (docs/DESIGN-PROD.md section 28.2, residual 5).
- Docs: DESIGN-PROD API-PROD-020 (the premise for chunks whose packet content is fixed when taken: the invalidation under the chunk
  write-lock too; the put-back once and in the finally block), section 15, section 28.2 residual 5, section 28.3 (:974, :979),
  change log; PLUGINS.md section 10 (a deferred chunk is sent in the tick its lock is free, maybe more than one tick later); TEST.md
  TC-PROD-679 steps 9 to 14, step 1 on what the count of the holder can miss, the line numbers and counts of the section.
- Test class (7 tests): two tests added after line 327, line 76 of the class comment changed in place, so the earlier <SB>:N
