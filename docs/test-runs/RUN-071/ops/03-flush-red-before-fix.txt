## why 9
- The CachedPacket the test queues first is taken by the write thread, whose supplier then waits: the thread is inside
  PacketWriting#writeQueue of one flushSync. While it waits the test queues 1,573 LoginPluginRequestPackets of 4,000 bytes (a frame of
  about 4,020 bytes each) and kicks: the LoginDisconnect goes behind them, online turns false, the closer thread starts its 1 s join.
- Released, the same writeQueue goes on filling its pooled buffer of 16,383 bytes: the held packet and 4 requests. Back in the loop the
  write thread sees the connection offline, runs flushSync once more (4 requests) and closes the channel: 9 packets, then the end of
  the stream. The other 1,569 requests and the disconnect packet stay in the queue.
- A pooled buffer another test grew can hold more (up to synaxis.max-packet-size, 2,097,151 bytes); the 1,573 requests are more than
  three of those, so two flushes never reach the disconnect packet whatever is in the pool.
