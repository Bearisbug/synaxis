World 1: the shard listed the player 282 ms before the client handled the join packet; the driver
read character.version and asked for the transfer at once, with no wait before the first transfer
(p10.py at f58514441e: ready = time.monotonic()). The ticket was issued 28 ms after join 1 and the
connection closed 61 ms after it, about one frame at the harness's 20 frames a second. No
END_CLIENT_TICK in world 1 saw a player, so the client wrote "state 1 none", and the judge counted 4
states in 5 worlds. Worlds 2 to 4 were each left 4.5 s to 5.1 s after their join (transfer_issued at
23:54:48.236, 23:54:53.827, 23:54:59.386), the --gap of 5.5 s from the answer to the previous
transfer less the arrival time.

World 5 had the same exposure at the other end: the driver created the done file right after the
poll that listed the player and the version read, at least 107 ms after join 5 in this run. The poll
runs every 0.5 s and the shard lists the player before the client's join (282 ms before in world 1),
so a poll landing earlier would have created the done file before join 5; the client checks it
every tick and would have ended with 4 joins ("the client joined 4 worlds, expected 5"). This is
read from the timing, not seen in a run.

The record condition and the judge are right. The fix is in the driver (code commit 6b07085d31): the
first transfer starts --gap seconds after the first character.version was read, and the done file is
created --gap seconds after the last transfer was answered, the wait it already kept between
transfers.
